# Y-model illustration rendering prompts

These prompts expand the brief Illustrations in §15 of the AI Aided Hive Engineering Proposal.

They provide rendering detail only. They do not define Engineering Domains, Layer names, processes, Product architecture, Project Profile defaults, or implementation bindings.

## Homologation

Show Product engineering as the consumer of information and homologation as a parallel Prescriptive Engineering Domain.

The information flow is pull-driven. Product engineering establishes a need and requests the homologation information required for that need. Do not depict homologation as pushing unsolicited information into Product engineering.

Show Domain-local Engineering Layers at several Magnifications without using mandatory Product/system/component names as model semantics.

At a coarser Product engineering context, show a request for programme-relevant certification information.

At a finer Product engineering context, show a separate request for implementation-relevant homologation information.

Show detailed homologation validation or test engineering remaining inside the homologation Domain while no coarser Product engineering need requires that detail.

Where a detailed homologation finding has a material coarser consequence, show the applicable information moving through adjacent homologation Layers before a same-Scale cross-Domain Exchange Item reaches Product engineering.

Place the requested Prescriptive-domain information on the Fixed side of the Y reconciliation.

Show feedback toward the homologation Domain as permitted, but do not imply that Product engineering can negotiate an economically convenient deviation from Fixed information.

Do not show a diagonal cross-Domain/cross-Scale Exchange Item.

## Accessibility and UX

Show a Prescriptive accessibility Domain, an Engineered UX Domain, and a receiving Product/front-end engineering context.

The receiving engineering context establishes separate information needs and pulls applicable information from each source Domain.

Place requested accessibility information on the Fixed side of the Y reconciliation.

Place requested UX information on the Negotiable side under normal conditions.

Show that a UX Decision may have provenance to Fixed accessibility information while the UX information itself remains Negotiable for the receiving reconciliation.

Where useful, also show the case in which applicable conditions make the Engineered UX information economically ineffective to change, so that flow is treated as Fixed for the current reconciliation.

Show feedback toward both source Domains.

For the Negotiable UX flow, feedback can lead to source-Domain reconsideration and a revised Exchange Item.

For the Fixed accessibility flow, feedback can report incompatibility or another consequence without creating a negotiated deviation in the receiving reconciliation.

Keep cross-Domain exchange at aligned Scale positions.

Keep cross-Scale propagation Domain-local and adjacent-Layer based.

Do not show diagonal Exchange Items or unsolicited Domain-to-Domain information pushes.
